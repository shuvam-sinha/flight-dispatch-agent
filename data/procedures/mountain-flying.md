# Mountain flying
category: operations

Terrain generates its own weather and its own hazards, and both are
strongest when the wind is strongest.

Cross ridges at a 45 degree angle rather than perpendicular, so a
downdraught encountered on the far side can be escaped by turning away
rather than by out-climbing terrain. Arrive at the ridge with altitude
already gained, typically 1,000 to 2,000 ft above it.

Expect updraughts on the windward side and downdraughts on the lee side
that can exceed the aircraft's climb rate. The correct response to sink
is to turn toward lower terrain, not to raise the nose.

Mountain wave and rotor produce severe turbulence well downwind of the
range and often in clear air. Lenticular clouds mark the wave; a ragged
rotor cloud beneath marks the worst turbulence.

Density altitude compounds all of it.
