# Oceanic operations
category: operations

Oceanic airspace has no radar coverage and no ground navigation aids.
Position is reported by the crew and separation is procedural, so
adherence to the cleared track, level and speed is a safety matter rather
than a courtesy.

Routes are defined by named lat/long waypoints -- 56N020W means 56 north,
20 west. Organised track systems across the North Atlantic and Pacific
are republished daily to follow the jet stream, so a crossing flown
westbound and eastbound on the same day uses different tracks.

Fuel planning must account for the absence of diversion options. Equal
time points and points of no return are computed before departure, and
extended-range twin operations carry rules about maximum diversion time
to a suitable airfield.

An aircraft without the range to cross with reserves has no fuel stop
available. There is nowhere to stop.
