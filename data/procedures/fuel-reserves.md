# Fuel reserve requirements
category: planning

Fuel planning starts from the destination, not the tanks. Compute what
the flight requires, add the reserve, and only then ask whether the
aircraft can carry it.

Under FAR 91.151, day VFR requires enough fuel to reach the first point
of intended landing plus 30 minutes at normal cruise power. Night VFR
requires 45 minutes. Under 91.167, IFR requires fuel to the destination,
then to the most distant alternate, then 45 minutes beyond that.

These are legal minimums, not planning targets. Headwinds stronger than
forecast, a hold, or a missed approach consume the reserve quickly.
Planning to land with an hour aboard is normal practice and costs almost
nothing on a typical flight.

Fuel exhaustion remains a leading cause of general-aviation accidents,
and almost every case was survivable at the planning table.
