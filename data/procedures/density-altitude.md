# Density altitude
category: performance
applies_when: high-elevation

Density altitude is pressure altitude corrected for temperature, and it
is what the aircraft actually feels. High elevation, high temperature and
high humidity each reduce air density, and their effects combine.

Consequences are simultaneous and all unfavourable: the engine produces
less power, the propeller is less efficient, and the wing needs a higher
true airspeed for the same lift. Takeoff roll lengthens, climb rate falls
and the service ceiling drops.

An airfield at 5,000 ft on a hot afternoon can behave like 8,000 ft or
more. Performance charts assume a standard atmosphere, so the correction
is not optional.

Plan departures for early morning where possible, reduce weight rather
than hope, and compute the actual takeoff distance for the actual
conditions against the actual runway length.
