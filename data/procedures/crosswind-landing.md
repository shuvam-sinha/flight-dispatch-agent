# Crosswind technique
category: operations

Every aircraft has a demonstrated crosswind component, established by a
test pilot on a good day. It is a demonstrated value rather than a
structural limit, but treating it as a personal limit is sound.

Two techniques are standard. The crab method points the nose into wind
and tracks the centreline, then removes the crab at the flare. The
wing-low method lowers the upwind wing and holds the centreline with
opposite rudder, which places the aircraft in the landing attitude
throughout.

Touch down on the upwind main wheel first and keep flying the aircraft
through the rollout: aileron progressively into wind as speed decays,
otherwise the wind can lift the upwind wing after landing.

Gusts matter more than the steady component. Add half the gust factor to
approach speed and plan for the strongest reported gust, not the average.
