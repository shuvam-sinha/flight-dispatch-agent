# High altitude cruise
category: operations
applies_when: high-altitude

Above 10,000 ft the physiological environment becomes the limiting
factor. Time of useful consciousness falls from around 20 minutes at
18,000 ft to well under a minute above 35,000 ft.

Hypoxia is dangerous because its onset is comfortable: euphoria, poor
judgement and slowed reaction arrive before any sense of breathlessness.
The victim often feels fine.

Know the aircraft's oxygen or pressurisation system and check it before
departure rather than when needed. In a pressurisation failure, the
sequence is oxygen mask on first, then an emergency descent, then
communicate.

Cruise altitude selection trades against wind. A higher level offers
lower fuel flow and better true airspeed, but the jet stream can impose a
headwind that outweighs both. The winds-aloft forecast decides which
level is fastest.
